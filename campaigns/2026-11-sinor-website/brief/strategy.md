## 1. What the site must achieve
Make a visitor in the marine and offshore sector able to answer, within one visit: what does this company do, is it credible and technically competent, and how do I reach a person. The site is a credible technical front door and an enquiry route. It is not a catalogue or a shop. It must say only what Rahul has stated, and show every gap honestly as a slot.

## 5. Positioning and tone
Positioning (draft, to be confirmed): a specialist marine and offshore technology company covering Dynamic Positioning and reference systems, marine electrical and automation, propulsion and thruster controls, with integration, upgrades, commissioning, service and technical support. The scope line is STATED by Rahul and not independently verified; the site should say "as stated by the company" nowhere visible but the strategy records it, and the Director keeps the source note in the build notes.
What makes it different: NEEDED FROM RAHUL. I do not invent a differentiator, number, year in business, fleet size or region served.
Tone: company voice, third person or "we" only for Sinor's own work and services (not for equipment of partners, see lessons 2026-11-06). Confident, accurate, calm, customer-focused, technically literate. Short sentences, plain statements.
Rules: no hype words (boost, revolutionary, world class, leading, cutting edge, best, unmatched), no "proud, thrilled, delighted", no claim numbers, no superlatives without evidence, no em dashes or en dashes. Marine terms follow the marine-technical-accuracy skill; do not expand acronyms without a source. Write "Dynamic Positioning" in full on first use per page, then "DP".

## 9. How the cinematic dark style serves this audience, and where it yields
Serves:
- Marine and offshore work is done at night, on a bridge, at sea. A deep navy-black stage with a single cool light (deck light, sonar ring, horizon line) reads as native to the sector (design-dna "Adapting the style" section) and sets a premium, serious tone suited to high-value technical buying.
- One focal visual per section with short headlines keeps the company message simple for a first-time visitor on a phone.
- The scroll scene can walk through the six stated capability areas as a structured story, giving a quick map of scope.
Must yield to readability for engineers (HYPOTHESIS that this audience reads for detail and scans for specifics):
- Body copy at least 16 px, weight 400 or more, contrast at least 4.5:1 on the dark stage (large text 3:1). Never put body text on the brightest part of a glow; use a scrim.
- Solutions and Service content sits on flat, calm dark panels with full text in the DOM. No content is revealed only by scroll animation.
- The canvas 2D scene is decorative (empty alt or aria-hidden), has a static fallback and a prefers-reduced-motion path showing the final state; the page works with JavaScript off.
- Tables, lists and headings stay plain and scannable; no condensed or light-weight display type for anything below headline size.
- The white logo plate is deliberate; the navy brand colour is not used as text on dark.
- Keep an optional light variant in the tokens so a print or reading view stays possible. Dark is a proposal for Rahul to approve (design-dna conflict table), not a settled brand rule.

## 10. Recommended stack
Confirm the brief: plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JS, multi-page with real URLs, shared css/ and js/ folders, system font stack, no CDN, no external requests, no tracker or cookie, no build step. Reasons: previews anywhere including a phone-driven cloud session, fast, easy to QA, good for SEO with real pages. No CMS, login, shop or search is needed, so no escalation is requested. The one non-default element is the canvas 2D scene in plain JavaScript, which needs no library; any 3D, WebGL or animation library still needs Rahul's approval (lessons 2026-11-06). Trade-off to state: with no CMS, Rahul or a developer must edit HTML to change content. Ask whether non-technical editing will be needed later, because that would change the stack. Hosting and publishing are untouched.

## 11. Risks
1. Conversion cannot complete: the form is unconnected and contact details are absent. Mitigate: say so in readable text, ask Rahul for an endpoint or address first.
2. Thin content: with no products, partners, projects or numbers, the site may feel generic and low in proof. Mitigate: precise, plain scope language and visible slots; resist filling gaps with invention.
3. Wrong company identity: name and base conflict between the profile and Rahul's other site. A wrong name in public copy is high cost. Mitigate: one token, resolved before publishing.
4. Repo is public: nothing from the private site, no personal contacts, no third-party names in files.
5. Dark style hurts legibility if the glow, thin type or grey text drift below contrast. Mitigate: tokens with computed ratios, contrast check in QA at all three viewports.
6. Motion and performance: canvas scene could harm LCP, INP or the 1.5 MB budget. Mitigate: lightweight canvas, static fallback first, test.
7. Unverified claims leaking: ISO, "15+ years, 100+ projects, 40+ countries", partner names. Mitigate: fact-reviewer pass on all copy against verified-facts.md.
8. Logo on dark shows as a white rectangle unless on a deliberate plate; recolouring is forbidden.
9. No analytics by default: success after launch cannot be measured beyond manual enquiry counts without a decision.
10. Legal exposure: a live contact form collects personal data; privacy text needs Rahul or legal sign-off (DRAFT NOT LEGAL ADVICE if drafted).
11. Audience is a hypothesis; the page order and emphasis may need to change once Rahul describes real buyers.
